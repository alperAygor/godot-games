## 2. Rule ambiguities and how they were resolved

All are implemented identically in both languages and covered by vectors.

1. **Drive direction.** `side` is relative to the loco's *current* cell. A drive continues "in the same direction":
   after the loco moves from cell p to q, the continuing side is the side of q opposite to p
   (`continue_side`). Cell side labels may flip along the track; that does not end a move.
2. **Head and free side.** The head is the far end of the train in the drive direction; its free side is the one
   not facing the next train vehicle (or `side` if the loco itself is the head).
3. **Couplings on a blocked step persist.** Pushing into a wagon that stands against a buffer couples it
   (`moved=false`, `coupled=[...]`, `blocked="buffer"`). Without this a wagon at a dead end could never be fetched.
   A step is **effective** if it moved or coupled something; effective steps are part of the drive.
4. **One branch decision per step.** Only the head at the start of the step may use `branch`. If a coupling
   makes a new head that faces a non-spring switch, the step ends right after coupling (`moved=false`,
   `blocked=""`); the next `step` call (same drive, same move) asks for the branch. `probe` reports
   `needs_branch` for the current head, so the view never needs to predict this.
5. **Foul (new block reason `"foul"`).** If the vehicle being pushed into stands on a switch and is already
   linked to a vehicle in the *other* branch on the side facing the head, linking would give it two links on one
   side (a tree). The step is blocked, nothing is coupled at that point. Earlier couplings of the same step stay.
6. **Self-bite** is checked against the whole train including vehicles coupled earlier in the same step.
7. **Invalid branch values** (not 0/1) are treated as 0. `side` outside 0/1 is a `"buffer"` block.
8. **Move counting (`count_moves`).** A drive action starting on the continuing side of the previous drive, with
   no uncouple in between, is the same move. A coupling-only drive counts as a move (it is a player drag).
   `steps` in a solution action is the number of *effective* step calls (coupling-only steps included).
9. **Links validated as chains**: at most one link per vehicle per cell side, linked vehicles adjacent, and no
   ring (a ring would satisfy the per-side rule but is not a chain).
10. **Solved** is a pure position check; train membership is irrelevant. A goal reached mid-drive counts
    (the player can stop there).

## 3. Normalisation used by the solver (why it is exact)

The fast model treats every vehicle that is not in the loco's train as **unlinked**. Uncoupling costs zero
moves, and a stationary link never makes anything cheaper: anything ahead gets coupled when pushed anyway; at a
switch an unlinked vehicle offers the same choice (coupling stop, rule 4) and can never foul. So min-moves is
unchanged. `solver.realize()` turns fast-model transitions into real actions and replays them with
`rules.py`, cutting interior links of detached groups when necessary, then `simplify_uncouples()` drops every
uncouple that is not needed for the replay to stay valid at the same move count. Same-colour wagons are merged
in the state key (occupancy bytes store colours only).

## 4. Search

* **Node** = (state, continuation side c), c in {a, b, "new move"}. Continuing in direction c costs 0, any
  other drive costs 1, uncoupling costs 0 and resets c.
* **Par** = exact backward 0-1 BFS from all solved states over the reversed explicit graph (`graph.py`).
  Measured: forward BFS *from* the goal as a stand-in for distance *to* the goal overestimated par by 0-4 moves
  (sample of 30 states on an Inglenook yard; e.g. approx 16 vs exact 9 at the "farthest" candidate), because
  coupling needs a push while uncoupling is free. The exact search fixed that.
* **Solution** = Dijkstra on (moves, cells) lexicographically, so `min_cells` is the fewest cells among
  optimal-move solutions. Cross-checked: the layered BFS and Dijkstra agreed on 30/30 random starts.

## 5. Metrics and difficulty

See §5 in the level table header of `docs/LEVELS.md` for the final formula; summary:

* `par`, `min_cells`, `states` (reachable from the start), `depth_hist` (exact distance-to-goal histogram over the
  reachable states, keys = moves), `dead_states` (reachable states from which the goal is unreachable).
* `detours`: moves of the stored solution after which some wagon is farther (pass-through track distance) from
  its nearest same-colour target than before the move.
* `solutions_at_par`: number of distinct optimal plans, counted as sequences of *wagon arrangements* at move
  ends (loco position and train membership ignored, so "stop one cell earlier" does not multiply plans; capped
  at 100000). This is an over-count where two projected steps are not realisable from the same full state;
  used only relatively.
* `difficulty` 1-10: see `solver.difficulty_score` and §8 below.

## 6. Mechanics: experiment and choice

`tools/yard/mechlab.py` takes three base yards with fixed wagon sets and adds each candidate mechanic in every
sensible placement, then computes the exact max par (loco starting alone). Raw output: `build/mechlab.json`.

| Base yard (base max par) | traction (power 1 / 2) | gate + plate best / median (12 placements) | spring best / median | fragile best / median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Inglenook 15 cells, 4 wagons (17) | **38** / 19 | 25 / 18 | 25 / 1 (4 placements) | 13 / 4 |
| Run-around 16 cells, 3 wagons (10) | **20** / 11 | **20** / 14 | 18 / 12 | 9 / 7 |
| Timesaver 20 cells, 3 wagons (14) | 21 / 15 | **32** / 20 | 23 / 17 | 12 / 10 |

Chosen (in `rules.py` and `yard_rules.gd`):

1. **Gate + pressure plate** (`kind: "gate"` / `"plate"` on cells). Gates are open while any plate cell holds a
   vehicle (evaluated before the step); a closed gate cell cannot be entered. Most consistent deepening: the
   median placement beats the base yard on all three yards.
2. **Traction limit** (`power` on the loco). A step that would move a train with more than `power` wagons is
   blocked `"traction"` (couplings stay, so the player sees what is attached and uncouples). Largest raw depth.
   Risk: power 1 can turn into pure shuttling, so levels using it are filtered by detours/plans, not by par alone.
3. **Spring switch** (`kind: "spring"`, `spring: branch`). Leaving through the branching side always takes the
   fixed branch; no choice (`probe.needs_branch=false`). Strong in the right place, but placement-sensitive (some
   placements make the yard unsolvable), so the generator only keeps solvable placements.

Rejected: **fragile wagon** (push-forbidden). It made every base yard *shallower* (it removes states instead of
forcing long plans). It was implemented in both languages for the experiment and then removed so exactly three
mechanics ship. Not tried, too invasive for milestone 1: turntable, crossing, second loco, crane, opposing wagons.

Extending: add the name to `CELL_KINDS`/`VEHICLE_KINDS` in both files, add the check in `step` in both, add the
block reason, regenerate vectors.

## 7. Generator

See `tools/yard/gen.py` docstring. Yards are hand-drawn track templates on the 7x11 grid (`layout.py` checks
bounds, edge lengths 1/√2, no crossing diagonals, max 45° bend per cell, switch frogs at 45°). For a template +
target assignment the full state graph is built from all goal states, exact distances computed backwards, and
starts with the loco alone are ranked by distance; the chosen start gets the full metric pass.

## 8. Difficulty formula (why)

`solver.hardness(m)` is the continuous score used for ordering and for the "no filler" check;
`difficulty` (1-10, in `index.json`) is a fixed linear mapping of it (`H_LO=3.0 -> 1`, `H_HI=17 -> 10`), not
rescaled per data set, so later worlds stay on the same scale.

    hardness = 0.30*par + 0.60*detours + 0.80*max(0, 3 - log10(plans)) + 0.50*max(0, log10(states) - 2)

* **par** is the main axis (plan length), but alone it rewards mindless length.
* **detours** (moves that take a wagon farther from its target) are what makes shunting puzzles hard for people:
  the right move looks wrong. Weighted 2x per unit compared with par.
* **plans@par** (distinct optimal plans by wagon arrangement): with thousands of equivalent optimal plans the
  player stumbles into one; with 1-10 the gate is narrow. Example: an Inglenook start with par 17 but 2544 plans
  scored lower than a par-15 start with 24 plans, and the generator picked the latter.
* **states**: tiny state spaces can be brute-forced by trial; log scale, small weight.

The weights are a judgement, not fitted to human data (none exists yet). The blind agent playtest is the first
external check; recalibrate when human data arrives.

**No-filler rule (owner, 2026-11-15):** inside every block (World 1 generated levels, each World 2 mechanic block)
each level must be at least `MARGIN = 0.3` hardness (about one par) above the previous one; this is enforced by
`tools/yard/check_levels.py` inside `verify.sh`. A block start (new mechanic) is a deliberate dip. The teaching
trio w1-001..003 must strictly increase in par. World 2 blocks are ordered so that each block's peak is higher
than the previous block's peak (traction, then spring, then gate, then mixed).

All figures: exact max par with the loco starting alone, same base yards and wagons as §6, 7x11 grid
constraints. Raw data: `tools/yard/lab_results.json` (from `mechlab.py` and `lab_mech.py`). Candidates beyond
the shipped three are implemented only in the lab (`tools/yard/lab_mech.py`, subclassing the fast model); the
shipped rules are untouched. Each would ship as a `kind` on cells/vehicles in both languages plus vectors.
